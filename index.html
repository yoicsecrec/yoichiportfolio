PROJECT 01

DIALOGUE ANIMATIC

During the making of this dialogue sequence, I made one major revision. The production time was fairly short, but I did not keep track of the exact dates, so I cannot give a clear start and finish date.

I made the sequence using Storyboarder, Blender, and Clip Studio Paint.

The main revision was about shortening the sequence. This is already the shortest version, but it still runs for over a minute. I cut some small silent movements and other moments that were not necessary, keeping only the beginning, development, and ending of the scene. I also wanted the sequence to end in a more cinematic way, so I built the final shots around that idea.

ROLE — Storyboard Artist / Animator
PROCESS — Beat analysis, acting passes, composition, timing
CAMERA — Shot size, eyelines, reaction holds, and emotional emphasis
SOFTWARE — Storyboarder, Blender, Clip Studio Paint

PROJECT 02

ACTION ANIMATIC (FULL)

I made this action animatic before I knew how to use Blender. Because of that, I created the backgrounds by taking 3D environments and simplifying them to fit the style of the storyboard.

The production took around one month. Since I was not working on another project at the time, I worked on it over a longer period and did not rush the process.

The animatic went through several revisions. I made two major revisions to the fight scene. I also changed one part of the scene where the NPC girl and the agent meet in the club. In the first version, the drunk girl was getting on top of the agent while he was trying to push her away. While this was happening, he noticed the target and ran after her. I changed this because I wanted to keep the quiet mood and rhythm of the chase scene before moving into the fight.

I also decided not to add sound. I tried creating dialogue and other sounds with open-source AI tools using apps such as Anaconda Prompt, but the results sounded too artificial to me. The same thing happened with effects such as gunshots when I tried working with them in DaVinci Resolve, so I decided to leave the animatic silent, just like my dialogue sequence.

I also left out music because of copyright. I knew some songs that would fit the atmosphere very well, but they were copyrighted, and I preferred to leave the animatic without music rather than use a free track that did not fit the scene.

ROLE — Storyboard Artist / Animator
PROCESS — Concept, staging, environment, character movement, edit and timing
CAMERA — Tracking, impact beats, screen direction, and clean geography
SOFTWARE — Storyboarder, Clip Studio Paint, DaVinci Resolve

PROJECT 06

SILENT STORYBOARD

A 14-page silent sequence focused on visual cause-and-effect, camera staging, rhythm, and readable storytelling without dialogue.

For this storyboard sequence, I used my own home as a 3D reference. I took photos of both the interior and exterior with my phone, choosing the shots based on each scene. I then simplified the photos and used them as backgrounds in the silent storyboard.

I wanted to tell the story without dialogue, so I focused on using movement and visual actions to communicate what was happening.

I also made one major revision. The original story was much longer, but I did not want the silent drama section to become too long, so I shortened the sequence and kept it focused.

This was the most intensive project I have worked on so far. I worked on it for about a week and a half, around 15 hours a day, including Saturdays and Sundays.

While making this sequence, I learned a lot about camera angles and visual staging. Out of all my projects, this one had the biggest impact on how I approach storyboarding.

PROJECT 07

KISS N DESTROY

A completed grayscale manga project that demonstrates sustained pacing, visual continuity, and long-form sequential storytelling.

I worked actively on Kiss N Destroy for two years. During that time, I also made one complete remake of the manga, redrawing and rebuilding every chapter and page from the beginning.

After working on the first version for a year, I felt that the storytelling and visuals were not at the level I wanted yet. I spent another year rebuilding the entire manga with a more cinematic approach, stronger visual storytelling, and cleaner page design, and then finished the final version.

Kiss N Destroy is not my first manga project. Before this, I worked on a much larger project that is over 600 pages long. Since it was drawn entirely by hand on paper, I decided not to include it in my portfolio.
